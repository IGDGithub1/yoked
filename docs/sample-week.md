# Sample Output — Week 5 Training Plan (v2)

Regenerated against the corrected spec: 5 **committed** days (§3.3a), core on every
strength day (§3.3b), synced as a buddy pair (§10).

## Scenario

- Week 5. Baseline fortnight done, 4 weeks of consistent adherence.
- **Buddy pair, synced.** Shared skeleton, individual prescriptions.
- **5 days/week stated capacity → 5 committed sessions.**
- Mon–Fri full gym, early morning, 60 min. Sat ≤3 hr, Sun 1 hr, outdoors, midday.

## Shared skeleton — 5 committed, 2 optional

| Day | Session | Focus | Status | Where |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mon** | Strength | Lower — squat + core | **Committed** | Gym, 60 |
| **Tue** | Strength | Upper — horizontal + core | **Committed** | Gym, 60 |
| **Wed** | Cardio + core | Conditioning | **Committed** | Gym, 60 |
| **Thu** | Strength | Lower — hinge + core | **Committed** | Gym, 60 |
| Fri | Strength | Upper — vertical + core | *Optional* | Gym, 60 |
| **Sat** | Cardio | Outdoor, long | **Committed** | Outdoors, ≤180 |
| Sun | Active recovery | — | *Optional* | Outdoors, 60 |

**5 committed. Hit those five and the week is a success — even if Friday and Sunday
never happen.**

### Why Friday is the optional one, not Wednesday

The obvious cut is the last day on the calendar. That's wrong here.

Wednesday carries U1's cardio, and **cardio is his lagging metric** — the thing he
needs most and wants least. Make it optional and he skips it every week, which
quietly deletes the goal. So Wednesday is committed and a *strength* day gives way.

Four strength days is more volume than either user needs at week 5; three plus real
conditioning serves both goals better. This is §3.3a's rule in practice: **cut by
goal value, not calendar position.**

Friday's vertical-push/pull work isn't lost — it rotates into the committed slots
across the block, and taking Friday when available is genuine upside.

# What the pairing produced

Where the shared skeleton held, and where it diverged (§10.2):

| Slot | U1 | U2 | Diverged? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon squat pattern | Goblet squat | Back squat | **Variant** — U1 re-grooving |
| Mon–Thu accessories | Same movements | Same movements | No — loads only |
| Tue press | Machine chest press | DB bench | **Variant** — tendon tolerance |
| Wed cardio | 25 min steady RPE 5–6 | 20 min intervals RPE 8 | **Prescription** — different bases |
| Thu hinge order | Trap bar leads | Hip thrust leads | **Order** — goal priority |
| Fri overhead | Machine press | DB press | **Variant** — hypertension caution |
| **Core blocks** | **Identical** | **Identical** | **No — shared by design (§10.2a)** |
| Sat/Sun | Together | Together | No |

Every divergence traces to a constraint or a goal — never convenience. They're in the
gym at the same hour doing the same session all five committed days.

**The core block is the deliberate convergence point.** Same exercises, same sets,
same reps, same holds — only loaded movements scale (60 lb vs 35 lb suitcase carry;
same movement, same distance). It's floor work, so nothing forces the prescriptions
apart the way a barbell does, and core strength converges far more than limb strength
anyway. Ten minutes of matched work on the mats at the end of each session is where
the pair actually talks, competes a bit, and messes about — which is the buddy
system's real mechanism, not a side effect of it.

## Format notes still worth folding into §3.3

1. **Core blocks land after the main work**, per §3.3b, with brief anti-rotation
   activation moved into the warm-up. Both visible above.
2. **Committed/optional is on every session header** — it has to be, or the
   distinction is invisible to the user and the whole point is lost.
3. **Loaded carries are doing double duty** — core work that's also grip and
   conditioning. Efficient in a 60-minute cap.
4. **§10.2's divergence-is-normal claim holds up.** Six divergences across five days,
   all constraint- or goal-driven, and the pair still trains together every session.
